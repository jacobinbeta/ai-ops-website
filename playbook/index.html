The CFO Buying Signals Playbook

10 signs a business needs a CFO, where to find them for free, and what to say when you do.

Jacob Krogue, CPA
Founder, AI Operations Partners

Before you start

Owners don't look for a CFO out of nowhere

I was the founding employee at a Fractional CFO firm and helped grow it from 8 clients to more than 30. Nearly all of them came through referrals. But some of our best work started with a signal.

We heard that a company had just opened a new location, so we reached out. They were in the middle of negotiating debt agreements and the capital and build-out terms for the new site, and they needed help. They became a multi-year client and kept growing and scaling with us.

Something usually happens before an owner goes looking for finance help. They open a location, take on debt, raise money or hire their first controller. Many of those moments leave a public trail. This playbook shows you where to find it, and what to say when you do.

Each signal comes with three things: why it matters, where to find it (free sources and the exact search to run) and what to say (a short first message to adapt to your own voice).

Short on time? Start with these three

  1. Signal 1, hiring a controller with no CFO: the clearest sign of need, and a two-minute search.
  2. Signal 3, opening a new location: set up two Google Alerts once, and leads arrive in your inbox.
  3. Signal 5, a recent raise: one free SEC search shows every new raise in your state.
Who to contact: at companies under about $50M in revenue, write to the owner, CEO or president. They feel the pain, and they make the decision.

Hiring signals

01

Hiring a controller with no CFO

Start here

Why it mattersThey've outgrown the bookkeeper and are building a finance team, with nobody above the controller to set direction. A controller closes the books. They don't build the forecast or manage the bank.

Where to find it
  • On LinkedIn Jobs or Indeed, search controller "reports to the CEO" in your area, posted in the last 30 days. Also try "reports to the owner" and "reports to the President".
  • On Google, search site:boards.greenhouse.io OR site:jobs.lever.co controller "reports to the CEO" to catch postings on company careers pages.

Hi [Name], I saw [Company] is hiring a controller who'll report to you. A good controller will tighten up the close. If you'd also like help with the forward-looking side, like cash forecasting and the bank, that's what my firm does part-time. Open to a 20-minute call?

02

Searching for a finance leader

Why it mattersA company posting for a CFO, VP or Head of Finance, especially to replace someone who left, has a gap right now. A full-time search takes months, and the close and the bank won't wait.

Where to find it
  • Search "Head of Finance" OR "Director of Finance" OR "VP Finance" in your area.
  • Prioritize postings that have been open 30 days or more. A long search means they're feeling the gap.

Hi [Name], I noticed [Company] is searching for a finance leader. While you find the right person, I can step in as fractional CFO so nothing slips. Some companies find part-time covers what they need for now. Worth a quick conversation?

Expansion and debt signals

03

Opening a new location

Start here

Why it mattersA new site usually means new debt, lease and build-out negotiations, new tax obligations, and an owner asking whether each location really makes money. That's exactly how our multi-year client started.

Where to find it
  • Set Google Alerts for "new location" OR "grand opening" [your state] and "breaks ground" OR "expansion" [your state].
  • Watch for the same company posting jobs in a new city.

Hi [Name], I saw [Company] is opening in [City]. Expansions like this usually come with new financing, lease and build-out terms to negotiate. I help owners get those right and see how each location performs. Would it help to talk?

04

Taking on new debt

Why it mattersLenders bring covenants and regular reporting. Many owners sign the loan and only then learn what the bank expects to receive.

Where to find it
  • The SBA publishes its 7(a) and 504 loan data on data.sba.gov, with borrower, city, lender, amount and approval date. Download the latest file and filter to your state and loan size.
  • Ask the commercial bankers you know. They see this signal first, and they want their borrowers to have good financial reporting.

Hi [Name], I saw [Company] recently closed financing with [Lender]. Banks often ask for more than owners expect, like covenant calculations and borrowing-base reports. If that's landing on your desk, I can take it off your plate.

Capital and growth signals

05

Just raised outside capital

Start here

Why it mattersNew investors expect board reporting and a credible forecast, often within the first quarter.

Where to find it

Companies that raise private money file a Form D with the SEC, usually within 15 days. It's free and lists the amount, the date and the executives.

  1. Go to sec.gov/edgar/search.
  2. Enter an industry keyword, set the form type to D, pick the last 30 days and set "Principal executive offices in" to your state.
  3. Skip funds and real estate vehicles, and focus on operating companies.

Hi [Name], I saw [Company] recently closed a round. The first board meeting after a raise is often when founders feel the reporting gap most. Happy to share the reporting package and forecast structure that works for companies at your stage.

06

Growing fast

Why it mattersFast growth burns cash even when the business is profitable, and the owner starts making big decisions without reliable numbers.

Where to find it
  • Filter the Inc. 5000 (inc.com/inc5000) by state and industry, and check your business journal's "fastest-growing companies" list.
  • On LinkedIn, look for companies whose employee count jumped over the past year.

Hi [Name], I saw [Company] on [List]. Growth like that is often when cash gets tight in ways the P&L doesn't show. I help companies at your stage build a cash forecast they can run the business on. Useful right now?

Change signals

07

Made an acquisition

Why it mattersIntegration means two sets of books, new systems and acquisition debt, which is more than most internal teams can absorb.

Where to find itSet a Google Alert for "acquires" OR "has acquired" [your state].

Hi [Name], I read about [Company]'s acquisition of [Target]. The first year of integration is when the numbers usually get messy. If you'd like an experienced hand on the finance side, I'd be glad to help.

08

New CEO or new owner

Why it mattersA new CEO, or an owner who just bought the business, wants a clear view of the numbers fast, and is open to new advisors in the first 90 days.

Where to find itSet Google Alerts for "named CEO" OR "appointed president" [your state], and watch local business news for ownership changes.

Hi [Name], congratulations on the new role at [Company]. Many new leaders want an independent view of the numbers before making big calls. I'd be glad to help you get there quickly.

Niche signals

09

Won a government contract

Why it mattersFederal contracts bring cost accounting and compliance rules that many growing contractors aren't set up for.

Where to find itOn USAspending.gov, use Award Search to filter for contracts awarded recently to companies in your state, then sort by amount.

Hi [Name], I saw [Company] won a contract with [Agency]. Government work comes with accounting requirements that catch a lot of contractors off guard. Happy to walk you through what to have in place.

10

A nonprofit growing fast

Why it mattersGrowth brings grant reporting, board scrutiny and, at $1 million or more in federal spending in a year, a Single Audit. Few can justify a full-time CFO.

Where to find itOn ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, search your state and look for big year-over-year revenue jumps in recent Form 990s.

Hi [Name], I noticed [Organization] has grown significantly in the last couple of years. That usually brings more grant reporting and board scrutiny. I provide part-time CFO support to nonprofits at your stage. Open to a short call?

Putting it to work

The 30-minute weekly routine

Signals only pay off if you check them every week. Block 30 minutes on the same day each week.

TimeWhat to do
10 minScan. Rerun your saved job searches (signals 1 and 2), check new Form D filings (signal 5) and skim your Google Alerts.
10 minQualify. Confirm each company fits your ideal client, has no CFO, and find the owner or CEO.
10 minReach out. Send 3 to 5 personal messages and log them.

Four rules

  • Lead with the signal. Your first line should show you noticed something specific about their business.
  • Move within two weeks. Timing is the whole advantage.
  • Follow up twice, about a week apart, adding something useful each time.
  • Keep it personal. Write each message to one person, say clearly who you are, and stop if they ask.
Track it in one spreadsheet: company, signal, date seen, contact, the dates of each touch, and the outcome.
